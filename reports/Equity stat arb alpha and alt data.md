# Fix turnover first, then add slower alphas

The 2023–2026 literature confirms what our paper 8 replication showed. A parametric residual-reversal book that trades fully to its signal every day does not survive 5 bp costs in US large caps. The fixes that work are about portfolio construction, not cleverer predictors. In the Attention Factors benchmark, PCA residuals with an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) threshold rule go from **gross Sharpe 1.26 to net −2.72**. On the same 500-stock universe, models that put the trading cost inside the training objective keep **net Sharpe 1.0–2.3** ([arXiv 2510.11616](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11616); [DLSA](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)). Our FF5 book sits at gross ~1.3 with 70–80% daily turnover and negative net Sharpe, which is exactly that failure mode. Fama-French residuals are also the weakest residual choice in the one paper that compares them directly (FF3 net −0.12, against PCA 0.57 and attention factors 1.45) ([arXiv 2610.25802](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25802)). Most of what the question asks about can be built with free data and the Python 3.13 venv we already have:

- cost-aware single-period and multi-period optimisation;
- PCA or characteristic residuals;
- a small cost-in-loss neural signal;
- a 20–30-signal alpha library with point-in-time EDGAR fundamentals;
- slow EDGAR-text and insider alt-data signals.

None of this needs MOSEK. Clarabel solves every continuous formulation, including 3/2-power impact, and MOSEK only becomes necessary for mixed-integer features such as cardinality or minimum trade sizes. Three things are poor uses of our time:

- LLM agentic alpha mining, whose best-controlled study finds certified alpha indistinguishable from zero after costs;
- options-skew signals, which have no free history;
- daily news sentiment, which decays with adoption and needs data we would have to start collecting now.

The recommended first build is a cost-aware optimiser wrapped around the existing paper 8 signals. That is about one to two days of work, costs minutes per backtest, and has a clear pass/fail test.

## Published stat arb splits on one variable: where turnover is controlled

The 2023–2026 stat-arb papers fall into a clean ranking once they are put on a common cost model of 5 bp per unit of turnover, 1 bp per day on short notional, and unit gross leverage. The Pelger group's Attention Factors paper runs on the 500 largest CRSP stocks out of sample over 1998–2021. It learns factors and trading policy jointly against net Sharpe and reports **gross Sharpe 3.97, net 2.28** ([arXiv 2510.11616](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11616)). Two-step pipelines are a step below: PCA residuals plus a neural signal reach **net Sharpe 1.57**. The parametric OU-threshold rule on the same residuals is the cautionary row, at **−2.72 net**. The 2021 DLSA paper got the same ordering earlier. With a cost-aware objective on IPCA residuals it reaches **net Sharpe 1.24 at K=3**, while its OU benchmark manages only 0.97 even before costs ([ar5iv 2106.04028](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)).

Two details in these papers matter directly for our redesign. First, the Attention Factors ablation shows that removing past-return characteristics cuts net Sharpe from **2.28 to 0.59** ([arXiv 2510.11616](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11616)). Most of the edge therefore runs through short-horizon return features, which we can compute from prices alone. Second, DLSA estimates the Sharpe **half-life at about 7 trading days**, with roughly half the Sharpe surviving a one-week hold ([ar5iv DLSA](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)). That makes our daily full rebalance needlessly expensive. The signal persists long enough to trade partially towards it over several days. The Korean NFE paper's no-tuning recipe makes the point bluntly: position proportional to the negative z-score, clipped at ±3, with **non-overlapping 20-day holds**, and PCA residuals still clear costs ([arXiv 2610.25802](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25802)).

Several popular alternatives look weaker on inspection. Korniejczuk and Ślepaczuk's graph-clustering multi-pair strategy reports an IR of 1.30 at 5 bp per side. The authors themselves flag that the result hinges on two volatile stocks. In their replication, the Cartea et al. clustering benchmark falls from **9.01% gross to 1.13% a year** at 5 bp ([arXiv 2406.10695](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10695)). The Polish deep-learning stat-arb paper is profitable in 2017–2019, but only the ETF variant survives 2020 ([arXiv 2512.02037](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02037v1)). I found no 2023–2026 paper with a net-of-cost US backtest of news-conditioned stat arb ([arXiv 2412.19245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19245)).

The honest caveat is decay. No paper in the notes isolates post-2015 residual-reversal profitability in US large caps, and the best paper's out-of-sample period ends in 2021. Our own paper 8 run shows the XGB baseline **negative in every year 2022–2026** ([our results](../research_notes/New%20trading%20strategies%20ML4T%20backtests/backtest_results_papers2026.md)). Practitioner support continues. Robeco's 2023 study finds residual and industry-adjusted reversal roughly doubles the risk-adjusted return of generic reversal and stays effective over time ([CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/reviving-short-term-reversal/)). Da, Liu and Schaumburg show that only the residual component of reversal profits is significant ([NY Fed SR 513](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr513.html)). Any rebuilt book must therefore report 2022–2026 separately, so that a cost fix cannot hide a dead signal.

## Cost-aware optimisation is the cheapest large lever, and Clarabel is enough

The arithmetic explains our negative net Sharpe. Daily turnover of 70–80% at 5 bp per unit of turnover costs about 0.75 × 5 bp × 252 ≈ **9.5% of gross notional a year**, or about 19% if each side is charged separately. A residual book with a gross Sharpe of 1.3 at single-digit volatility cannot absorb either figure. The paper 8 authors' own models traded only **13–27% a day**, which is why their net Sharpe was 0.58–0.62. Our stabilised exposure rules cut turnover to about 45% and added up to **+1.19 net Sharpe** (p < 0.001), but still left the book below zero ([our results](../research_notes/New%20trading%20strategies%20ML4T%20backtests/backtest_results_papers2026.md)). The direction is right. It just did not go far enough.

The optimisation literature shows how far it can go. Gârleanu and Pedersen's optimal policy is to "aim in front of the target" and trade partially towards it, with slower-decaying signals getting more weight in the aim ([NBER w15205](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15205)). Boyd and co-authors' 2024 "Markowitz++" backtest runs on 74 S&P 100 names over 2000–2023 with realised spreads and a synthetic IC-0.15 forecast. It raises Sharpe from **0.19 to 4.32** and cuts annual turnover from **1,145× to 28×** by adding trade-cost, holding-cost, leverage and robustness terms ([arXiv 2401.05080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05080)). The forecast there is synthetic and the universe survivor-biased, so the magnitude will not carry over. The mechanism will. The 2017 cvxportfolio paper calls the trade-aversion parameter "critical". It finds the best γ_trade around 5–7, finds large holding-cost multipliers useful as regularisation, and shows a 2-day multi-period horizon sitting well above single-period optimisation ([arXiv 1705.00109](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.00109)). Baldi-Lanfranchi (2024) find partial rebalancing raises net maximum squared Sharpe **by up to 2.5×**, with most of the gain in high-turnover factors that are "otherwise unprofitable net of costs" ([AFA](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=135184)). That description fits our book.

The formulation for a 300–500-name dollar-neutral book is a single-period convex problem. It maximises alpha'w minus three penalties:

- a factor-model risk term, with Σ = FΣ_fF' + D;
- linear spread cost plus 3/2-power impact on trades;
- a borrow cost on shorts.

It is subject to these constraints:

- dollar, beta and sector neutrality;
- FF5 exposure bounds;
- position limits;
- participation limits of |z| ≤ 5% of volume;
- an optional turnover cap.

That is an SOCP or power-cone problem ([arXiv 2401.05080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05080); [MOSEK Cookbook ch. 6](https://docs.mosek.com/portfolio-cookbook/transaction.html)). MOSEK solves the 500-asset, 20–50-factor version in **0.07–0.10 s**, and the Boyd paper reports that Clarabel was only "a bit longer" ([arXiv 2401.05080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05080)). CVXPY's solver table lists Clarabel with LP, QP, SOCP, SDP and exponential and power cones, but no mixed-integer support ([CVXPY docs](https://www.cvxpy.org/tutorial/solvers/index.html)). That settles the licence question raised earlier. Everything we need for the next experiments runs on the cvxpy and Clarabel already installed. MOSEK earns its licence in three cases:

- mixed-integer features: cardinality, fixed costs, or buy-in thresholds via binaries;
- numerical trouble on tight neutrality constraints;
- very large hyperparameter sweeps, where a few-fold speedup matters.

Installing it is easy if we do want it. `pip install mosek` gives 11.2.6, which supports Python 3.9–3.14 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/Mosek/json)), and the licence file goes at `C:\Users\Acer\mosek\mosek.lic` ([MOSEK licensing](https://docs.mosek.com/latest/licensing/quickstart.html)). The free Personal Academic licence requires an academic email. Anyone else gets a 30-day trial ([MOSEK academic](https://www.mosek.com/products/academic-licenses/); [trial](https://www.mosek.com/products/trial/)). The trial is best spent on a single benchmarking week once the Clarabel pipeline is finished. The MOSEK Portfolio Cookbook (v1.6, free PDF) is worth reading regardless of solver, because its transaction-cost and multi-period chapters are solver-agnostic modelling recipes ([Cookbook index](https://docs.mosek.com/portfolio-cookbook/index.html)).

Two tooling notes from the PyPI check. cvxportfolio 1.5.1 is pure Python and implements `MultiPeriodOptimization` directly, but it is GPLv3. skfolio 1.9.7 and Riskfolio-Lib 7.4.0 are BSD-3 and support Python 3.13 ([PyPI JSON](https://pypi.org/pypi/zipline-reloaded/json); [skfolio](https://skfolio.org/)). For a trade-level stat-arb book, hand-written CVXPY with `cp.Parameter` objects is simpler than either library. It also avoids recompiling the problem on each of roughly 4,000 backtest days. The one risk to plan around is in the mechanism itself. If the residual alpha decays within a day, smoothing trades will also smooth away the alpha. The ~7-day half-life in DLSA suggests that will not happen, but our own signals must be checked by measuring IC decay by lag before tuning.

## Alpha signals: combinations and cost-aware construction beat single anomalies

The big replication studies from 2023–2026 send a sobering message for anyone building a standalone signal. Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen find that most of 153 factors replicate across 93 countries ([NBER w28432](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)). Usefulness to an investor after costs is a different matter. Chen and Velikov put the average anomaly's net expected return at about **4 bp a month**, about 20 bp for combinations ([JFQA 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225)). Chen and Welch find the median non-microcap anomaly earned **7 bp a month gross after 2005**, small enough that modest allowances for costs eliminate it ([arXiv 2607.06502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06502)). The counterweight is ML combination with cost-aware construction. Azevedo, Hoegner and Velikov find that costs and decay cut ML strategy returns by about 57% on average. The best strategies still net up to **1.42% a month** out of sample, and an LSTM has a six-factor net alpha of 1.20% a month (t = 3.46) ([Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/the-expected-returns-of-machine-learning-strategies/)). Jensen, Kelly, Malamud and Pedersen show that learning portfolio weights against a net-of-cost objective beats ranking by predicted return, because cost-blind forecasts lean on short-lived characteristics ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/machine-learning-and-the-implementable-efficient-frontier)). Gu, Kelly and Xiu's models all agree that momentum, liquidity and volatility dominate ([NBER w25398](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf)).

For us, this argues against hunting for a single new anomaly. A library of roughly 20–30 standard signals should be combined into a slow composite. That composite then serves as the "aim" alongside the fast residual signal, which is Gârleanu-Pedersen's point that slow signals deserve more weight. Qlib's Alpha158 operator set gives exact, code-ready price/volume formulas ([Qlib loader.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/qlib/main/qlib/contrib/data/loader.py)). Kakushadze's 101 Formulaic Alphas adds short-horizon variants ([arXiv 1601.00991](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.00991)). Open Source Asset Pricing reproduces 319 predictors and their code, which makes it the reference for fundamental definitions ([SSRN 3604626](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3604626)). Qlib itself publishes no Python 3.13 wheel, only cp38–cp312 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/pyqlib/json)). We should transcribe the formulas into polars rather than install it, or keep it in a separate 3.12 environment.

Free fundamentals come from SEC EDGAR's keyless XBRL APIs, with nightly bulk `companyfacts.zip` files and a fair-access limit of 10 requests per second with a declared User-Agent ([SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces); [SEC access](https://www.sec.gov/os/accessing-edgar-data)). One trap is critical. The `frames` endpoint returns each filer's *latest* value for a period, which builds restatements into the history and creates look-ahead. A point-in-time panel must instead be built from `companyfacts`, keeping the first-filed value per concept and period ([SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)).

On agentic or LLM alpha mining, the best-controlled 2026 study is a frozen-referee, e-process walk-forward on CSI 500 at 15 bp. It finds the certified factor book had alpha **indistinguishable from zero**, and the median wait to certify a true factor was about 500 trading days. Its IC-based gates "favour short-horizon reversal, which does not pay after costs" ([arXiv 2609.27051](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27051v1)). EverMine's long-horizon agent shows no consistent gain from evolving capabilities ([arXiv 2609.33524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33524)). AlphaAgent's S&P 500 IR of 1.05 is self-reported, and its test window overlaps the LLM's training data ([arXiv 2502.16789](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v1)). The transferable lesson is the governance recipe, not the agent:

- log every candidate tried;
- deduplicate by correlation;
- freeze the evaluator;
- judge net of costs;
- deflate for the full trial count.

Competition winners add operational discipline that transfers to our setting. Optiver 2023's winning entry was a CatBoost/GRU/Transformer blend with zero-mean re-centring of predictions per timestep and refitting during the test period ([Zhihu notes](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/689864294)). In Jane Street 2024–25, a single online gradient step on each newly labelled batch added about +0.008 to CV R² ([8th-place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/c/jane-street-real-time-market-data-forecasting/writeups/evgeniia-grigoreva-private-lb-8th-solution)). Numerai's feature-neutral correlation (FNC) projects predictions off the feature matrix before scoring, a roughly 20-line numpy step that guards against factor-regime shifts ([Numerai FNC](https://docs.numer.ai/numerai-tournament/scoring/feature-neutral-correlation)). Carver's 2026 posts argue for heavy shrinkage when fitting combination weights, at SR shrinkage 0.5 and correlation shrinkage 0.75 ([qoppac](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/)). Our ML pipeline's real but thin IC of 0.016–0.029 is exactly the kind of signal these techniques are designed for.

## Free alt data favours slow EDGAR signals; fast ones need years of self-collection

The alt-data evidence divides by speed, and speed decides what we can test today. LLM news sentiment has the most 2023–2026 out-of-sample work. Lopez-Lira and Tang show GPT-4 scores on post-cutoff headlines predict the drift after the initial reaction, mostly in small caps and after negative news. They also show returns falling as LLM adoption rises ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v5)). Kirtac's 2026 follow-up uses 5 bp costs and a 10% ADV cap and evaluates only post-cutoff news. Decoder LLMs survive that test, while the dictionary baseline loses money after turnover ([arXiv 2609.23703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23703)). We cannot test this credibly with free data. Free RSS feeds have no history and GDELT maps poorly to tickers. Post-cutoff-only evaluation, the standard Kirtac adopts against memorisation look-ahead, also limits any backtest on current models to roughly one or two years. The realistic move is to **start archiving headlines and option chains now** and treat news as a 2027–2028 research line.

Slow signals are testable today. Lazy Prices, the 10-K/10-Q language-change signal, earned up to 188 bp a month in alpha in the original JF 2020 paper, with no announcement-day reaction ([EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/lazy-prices-cohen-lauren/10012910642)). A practitioner version beat SPY on a risk-adjusted basis from 2020 to mid-2026 ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/20966/filing-language-stability-as-a-selection-signal/)). It trades monthly or quarterly, needs only EDGAR text, and is therefore the most cost-robust alt-data choice we have. Opportunistic insider purchases (Form 4, code P) are free too, but a 2022–2026 replication finds only **+1.01% CAR over 5 sessions**, with longer-horizon intervals including zero ([QuantInsti](https://blog.quantinsti.com/sec-form-4-insider-trading-python-event-study/)). FINRA daily short-sale volume is free from August 2018 ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/reg-sho-daily)). Only the liquidity-supplying component predicts returns ([Quantpedia on Stealthy Shorts](https://quantpedia.com/short-sellers-informed-liquidity-suppliers/)), and older work finds trading costs kill a daily version ([NBER draft](https://users.nber.org/~confer/2005/mmf05/werner.pdf)). Use it weekly or monthly. Option-skew predictability falls by at least two-thirds once high-borrow-fee stocks are excluded ([Mendeley replication](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/n73cyx89gs)), and yfinance offers only current chains, so it is not worth pursuing. A 2026 supply-chain paper propagates FinBERT MD&A embeddings over a customer-supplier graph and reports Sharpe 0.86 and FF5 alpha of 7.27% a year, with no cost analysis ([arXiv 2606.29290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29290)). FinBERT runs on our torch CPU install, so this is feasible but second-tier.

The one alt-data idea that plugs directly into our stat-arb book is unpublished: fade residual moves that come *without* firm-specific news, and stand aside on moves that come *with* news. The literature motivates it, since news drifts while no-news shocks revert ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v5)). But no 2023–2026 paper tests it net of costs, so it is a hypothesis, not a recipe. It also depends on the news archive we have not yet built.

The plan below builds on the paper 8 code in `strategies/papers2026/` and on the ml4t-diagnostic deflated-Sharpe and walk-forward tools already verified on this machine. Every step is judged on net Sharpe at 5 bp per unit of turnover plus 1 bp a day on shorts, the published standard. Every step also reports 2022–2026 separately and logs all variants for a deflated Sharpe. The pre-registered target throughout is **net Sharpe ≥ 0.5 with deflated-Sharpe probability ≥ 0.9 across all logged trials**, plus **positive net Sharpe in 2022–2026**.

Priority 1 comes first because it reuses everything we already have and isolates the one variable the literature says matters. It also gives a quick verdict. If no γ_trade, band or holding period lifts the paper 8 book above zero net, the residual signal itself is too fast or too decayed. In that case priorities 2–3 should start from PCA residuals rather than FF5. Priority 4 is the most labour-intensive and the most likely to yield something durable: Azevedo-Hoegner-Velikov's net results come from combining many published anomalies, and that kind of composite is slow enough for an optimiser to trade cheaply. Survivorship bias runs through all of it, because we use current S&P 500 members. Our earlier report found no free point-in-time constituent source for any of the indices we tested apart from a China candidate, and yfinance usually lacks prices for delisted tickers. Survivorship flatters reversal strategies, so a positive result here is an upper bound until a point-in-time universe is sourced.

## Conclusion

The research changes where effort should go. Our paper 8 result is not an implementation failure. It reproduces a published benchmark (parametric reversal on factor residuals, traded daily) that every 2023–2026 paper with a cost model also finds unprofitable. The papers that clear costs differ from us in two specific ways we can copy: they choose better residuals, and they put turnover inside the objective. Neither needs paid data, a GPU or MOSEK. The gap between our gross ~1.3 and a credible net figure is mostly an engineering problem, and it can be resolved within days.

The larger strategic point is that our fast and slow signals have been tested in separate silos, and both failed on costs alone. The literature's most consistent net-of-cost finding is that combining them helps: an optimiser that leans on a slow composite and lets the fast residual signal time the trades. A residual-reversal book with a cost-aware optimiser, a slow fundamental and EDGAR-text composite as the aim, and a deflated-Sharpe log of every variant is the most promising thing to build with free data in 2026. If even that fails in 2022–2026, the evidence says large-cap US residual reversal has been arbitraged away, and research effort should move to the slower EDGAR signals.
