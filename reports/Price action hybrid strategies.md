# Filters, not new patterns, add the edge

The price-action rules with real evidence for US stocks and ETFs are still the few found in the first round: **daily oversold-in-uptrend mean reversion (RSI(2), IBS, short pullbacks), intraday Noise Area momentum with a VWAP trailing stop, and slow trend filters**. What this round adds is evidence on the *combinations*, and it is clear about what the extra ingredient does. Volume earns its keep when it **selects what to trade**: opening relative volume takes a plain 5-minute opening-range breakout from Sharpe 0.48 to 2.81 on "Stocks in Play", and an independent rebuild got 2.40. A VWAP trailing stop takes SPY intraday momentum from Sharpe 0.61 to 1.24. Volatility and regime overlays, including the out-of-sample statistical jump model (Sharpe 0.48 to 0.68 and max drawdown −55% to −27% on the S&P 500 net of costs) and volatility targeting, **mostly cut drawdowns and tails rather than adding return**. Seasonal edges (pre-FOMC, turn of month) and cross-asset canaries are alive but weakened or decaying. Volume confirmations such as OBV and CMF, squeeze breakouts, same-day VIX term-structure switches, and the much-marketed VWAP-only QQQ rule have no credible support. Among the extra pure price-action rules, Double 7s and the overnight hold have support; most Connors variants are book specifications without fetched statistics. Published results suggest expecting a **one-third to one-half Sharpe haircut out of sample**. The best rules therefore have to be chosen by a pipeline that is stricter than the backtests that produced them: full cost modelling with 2x and 3x stress and a break-even cost, a trial ledger feeding the Deflated Sharpe, Hansen SPA and Romano-Wolf StepM family tests, PBO from CSCV, bootstrap confidence intervals, robustness gates, and a sealed holdout run once. A rank-based scorecard weighted towards holdout and deflated statistics then picks a small, low-correlation set rather than one noisy winner. On the earlier round's own numbers, the only rule that already passes that bar is Noise Area plus VWAP stop on QQQ (out-of-sample Sharpe 1.31). The best use of hybrids is to repair or diversify what already works, not to search for new patterns.

Two patterns run through the table. First, an added ingredient helps when it has a mechanism that matches the base rule. RVOL picks stocks with news-driven order flow, and the VWAP stop cuts losing momentum days early. A regime filter avoids crash periods when long-only rules bleed. Second, the rules with the most impressive in-sample buckets (VIX > 40 at Sharpe 3.5, VWAP-only at Sharpe 2.1) are exactly those with no out-of-sample support, which is the signature of selection from many cuts of the same data.

## Volume earns its place intraday as a selector and a stop

### Relative volume turns a dead breakout into the best-documented day-trading rule

The cleanest "pure price versus price plus volume" comparison in the literature is the Stocks-in-Play opening-range breakout of Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz. Each day it screens all US stocks (more than 7,000, 2016–2023) for open above $5, 14-day average volume of at least 1 million shares and 14-day ATR above $0.50. It then computes **relative volume as the first 5-minute bar's volume divided by the 14-day average of that same bar**, requires at least 100%, and trades only the top 20. A bullish first bar places a buy-stop at the 5-minute high, a bearish one a sell-stop at the low. The stop is 10% of ATR14, the position exits at the close, risk is 1% of capital per trade with a 4x leverage cap, and commissions are $0.0035 per share ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=45379)). The filtered portfolio reports **more than 1,600% total net return and Sharpe 2.81**, while the same breakout on all eligible stocks earns Sharpe 0.48 ([Let's Data Science summary](https://www.letsdatascience.com/news/traders-adopt-orb-strategy-for-stocks-in-play-19394da2); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html)). A QuantConnect recreation reports **Sharpe 2.40 with beta −0.04**, against 0.84 for SPY buy-and-hold ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)). The contrast with index instruments is decisive. An ORB replication on five equity indices over 2015–2026 found "gross reproduced, net zero" ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)), and the earlier in-house QQQ ORB broke even at 2.24¢ per share of slippage, matching an independent GitHub replication to the tenth of a cent ([prior backtest results](../research_notes/Price%20action%20strategies%20by%20horizon/backtest_results_price_action.md)). The edge is in the selection, not the breakout. The remaining doubts are practical: no 2025–2026 post-publication test exists, the results are in-sample, and a fair test needs survivorship-free minute bars for every listed stock plus realistic spreads at 9:35.

### The VWAP stop, not the entry, carries the Noise Area gain

Concretum's "Beat the Market" paper gives a fully specified intraday momentum system, and its own ablation shows where the value lies. For each minute of day, σ is the 14-day mean of |Close(HH:MM)/Open(9:30) − 1|. The upper band is max(Open, prior Close) × (1 + σ) and the lower band is min(Open, prior Close) × (1 − σ). Signals are checked only at HH:00 and HH:30, the position flattens at the close, and sizing is shares = AUM × min(4, 2%/σ_SPY,14d) / Open ([Beat the Market PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)). On SPY from May 2007 to April 2024, with $0.0035/share commission and $0.001/share slippage, the breakout with a band stop earns **Sharpe 0.61 with skew −1.24**. Making the stop max(upper band, VWAP) for longs and min(lower band, VWAP) for shorts lifts it to **Sharpe 1.24 with skew +1.29** and halves the worst day from −10.3% to −4.8%. Volatility-targeted sizing adds only a further 0.09, to **Sharpe 1.33, 19.6% a year and 25% MaxDD** ([Beat the Market PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)). The same rules give Sharpe 2.03 on QQQ and 1.48 on SOXX in the authors' conference slides ([MoneyShow deck](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Money_Show_Final.pdf)), and across 33 futures the average Sharpe is 0.60 with only two negatives ([PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)).

The earlier in-house test turns this into a mixed verdict. On QQQ the 1x VWAP-stop version earned **Sharpe 1.31 over March 2024 to October 2026**, the window after the paper's sample, with break-even slippage above 3¢ per share. On SPY it reproduced in-sample (0.99) but earned **Sharpe 0.00 out of sample, and −0.99 in 2026 to date** ([prior backtest results](../research_notes/Price%20action%20strategies%20by%20horizon/backtest_results_price_action.md)). Two of the paper's own conditioning results are the natural repair candidates for SPY. Sharpe rises from about 1.5 on all days to **about 3.5 when VIX at the open is above 40**, and on days after an NR4 (narrowest range of four days) the strategy averages **+22 bp a day with t = 5.14**, the strongest of the day patterns tested ([PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)). Both are in-sample buckets, and the VIX > 40 bucket covers few days, so any threshold must be chosen inside a walk-forward loop. Gao, Han, Li and Zhou's finding that market intraday momentum is stronger on high-volume, high-volatility and macro-news days points the same way ([JFE 2018, RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)). Gating, however, cannot rescue the plain last-half-hour rule: it was negative on both SPY and QQQ over 2016–2026 even before costs ([prior backtest results](../research_notes/Price%20action%20strategies%20by%20horizon/backtest_results_price_action.md)), a 2024 study reports that intraday momentum has diminished ([MDPI Risks](https://mdpi.com/2227-9091/12/11/180)), and CXO measured R² of about 0.014 on 2012–2014 data with moves too small to exploit ([CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/first-and-last-half-hours-of-trading-linked)).

### VWAP as an entry signal fails, and the overnight hold is contested

The 2023 "VWAP Holy Grail" rule (long QQQ above VWAP, short below) reports $25k growing to $192,656, **Sharpe 2.1 and MaxDD 9.4%** over 2018–2023, but it does not publish bar size, signal frequency or trade count ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/volume-weighted-average-price-vwap-the-holy-grail-for-day-trading-systems/)). A QuantConnect implementation shows **Sharpe −0.6 and a 49% drawdown at 2,108% turnover** ([QuantConnect report](https://www.quantconnect.com/reports/2dd44a5c1394e0ba3de9c7f4d7dc0716)), and a forum replication found poor raw results only partly improved by a 2% deadband ([QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/16706/bear-bull-traders-039-paper-on-quot-the-holy-grail-quot-not-so-fast/)). The contrast with the Noise Area ablation is instructive: VWAP is useful for deciding when a momentum day has failed, not for flipping a position on every cross.

The overnight effect is the one pure intraday rule where the two research rounds disagree, and the disagreement narrows to costs. Haghani, Ragulin and Dewey estimate a stock-level overnight-minus-intraday long-short at about **38% a year before costs**, with borrow costing only about 1 point ([JOIM 2024](https://joim.com/wp-content/uploads/emember/downloads/P0753.pdf)). RobotWealth finds the SPY split persisted through May 2024 ([RobotWealth](https://robotwealth.com/revisiting-overnight-vs-intraday-equity-returns/)), and a 2025 paper attributes nearly all 30-year equity gains to overnight hours ([arXivLens summary](https://arxivlens.com/PaperView/Details/does-overnight-news-explain-overnight-returns-3036-75a58faf)). Against that, CXO finds the outperformance "not stable over time" ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=27932)), the E-mini 2–3 a.m. drift averaged about zero over 2021–2025 ([NY Fed Liberty Street](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)), and trading costs wipe out the SPY close-to-open trade ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/trading-costs-wipe-out-the-overnight-return-anomaly/)). The honest position is that the gross pattern exists but a daily round trip through two auctions consumes it; it deserves a test only with auction slippage modelled explicitly. A related mechanical flow, leveraged-ETF and option-hedging rebalancing into the close, produces end-of-day momentum or reversal that reverts next day ([Barbon et al., SSRN 3925725](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3925725)). It overlaps with the failed last-half-hour rule and should be tested only as a conditional enhancement.

## Swing mean reversion gains from gates, but the gates are mostly untested

### Additional pure rules mostly duplicate RSI(2)

Of the extra daily rules searched for, only **Double 7s** has a usable independent test. It buys SPY when the close is above the 200-day SMA and is the lowest close of seven days, and sells at a seven-day-high close, with no stop. A vendor test on SPY from January 1993 to September 2026 shows **333 trades, PF 2.35, Sharpe 0.85, MaxDD about 12.5% and about 28% exposure, at zero costs** ([StrategyQuant](https://strategyquant.com/blog/larry-connors-double-7-strategy-tested-on-spy-and-8-other-markets/)). An independent Python test over 1990–2024 found that replacing the seven-day-high exit with close > SMA5 was more consistent across lookbacks ([Ayrat Murtazin](https://ayratmurtazin.beehiiv.com/p/backtest-results-for-larry-connors-double-7-strategy)). That makes Double 7s structurally the same pullback-in-uptrend trade as RSI(2) with an SMA5 exit, so it should be treated as a robustness variant that will correlate highly with the existing book, not as a diversifier. Williams %R "seems to work pretty well" on SPY according to QuantifiedStrategies, but its rules are paywalled ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/larry-williams-r-trading-strategy)), and the Larry Williams open-plus-k×range breakout has only vendor claims without figures ([WH SelfInvest](https://www.whselfinvest.com/en-be/trading-platform/free-trading-strategies/tradingsystem/56-volatility-break-out-larry-williams-free)). Cumulative RSI, the %b strategy, TPS scaling-in, turnaround Tuesday and key reversals had no fetched statistics this round. They can be coded from their book definitions as additional trials, but every one of them must be logged in the trial count, since each is another cut of the same SPY pullback data.

The earlier in-house results set the bar these variants must clear. Across 15 ETFs at 6 bp per side in the 2016–2026 holdout, the median Sharpe was **0.37 for RSI(2), 0.42 for RSI(2) plus IBS < 0.2, 0.44 for three down days and 0.61 for IBS alone**, against 0.72 for buy-and-hold. Next-open fills cost 0.05–0.2 Sharpe versus close fills, and **NR7, inside-bar, hammer and engulfing setups showed no edge** ([prior backtest results](../research_notes/Price%20action%20strategies%20by%20horizon/backtest_results_price_action.md)). Profit factors of 1.5–1.9 with about 70% winners confirm the edge exists, but at 5–12% time in market it cannot beat holding the index in a bull decade. Gates that raise the return per trade, or let the rule trade bigger when the edge is largest, are therefore the right hybrid target.

### VIX gates fit the mechanism, but inverse-vol sizing works against it

Connors' **VIX Stretches** rule buys SPY at the close when SPY is above its 200-day SMA and VIX has closed at least 5% above its 10-day SMA for three or more days, and exits when SPY's RSI(2) closes above 65 ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/emini-emicro-index/18571-using-vix-time-markets.html); [book PDF](https://thechartist.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/504/ShortTermTradingStrategiesThatWork.pdf)). Public evidence is thin. One forum test "did rather well" over 1997–2011 without numbers, and a related VIX-spike rule had only 18 trades ([Schaeffer's](https://www.schaeffersresearch.com/content/news/2014/12/12/how-to-trade-a-surging-vix-by-the-numbers)). A QuantifiedStrategies VIX rule reports **328 trades, 75% wins, PF 2 and 5.2% CAGR** with paywalled rules ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/vix-trading-strategy-for-the-s-and)), and Concretum's 2026 "volatility burst" rule reports **10.8% CAGR net at 14% exposure** over 2007–2026, also with paywalled rules ([Concretum Substack](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/a-profitable-strategy-for-short-term)). The one direct test of a volatility filter on RSI(2) found only a "small, non-significant" improvement ([R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2010/09/mean-reverting-strategies-and-volatility/)).

The more important implication is about sizing. Bounce size in mean reversion scales with volatility, so ATR or inverse-volatility sizing shrinks exactly the trades that pay most. Volatility targeting is well supported for trend and momentum books, where it raises equity Sharpe modestly and thins the left tail ([Harvey et al. 2018](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). For mean reversion, fixed-fraction sizing or VIX-increasing sizing with a hard cap is the logical hybrid. That is an inference to test head-to-head, not a published result. Volume has a theoretical case here too. Llorente, Michaely, Saar and Wang show that in large, tight-spread stocks high-volume days are followed by **reversal rather than continuation** ([RFS 2002, NYU archive](https://archive.nyu.edu/jspui/bitstream/2451/26698/1/FIN-00-032.pdf)), which predicts that a high-volume down day should strengthen an RSI(2) or IBS entry on SPY. No published test exists, and a Quantopian attempt to sort mean-reversion entries by relative volume found no improvement ([Quantopian archive](https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/threads/enhancing-short-term-mean-reversion-strategies-1)). OBV, Chaikin Money Flow, up/down volume and price-volume divergence have no published add-on tests at all, so any such rule enters with every parameter free.

Two volatility rules should be dropped. Holding SPY only when VIX3M/VIX is above 1.0 produced an **information ratio of −0.55 and −6.7% a year of active return** ([GitHub](https://github.com/Filip303/Simple-Strategy)), and a 2016–2025 check found backwardation on about 7.6% of days with no reproducible bearish signal; what information exists sits in the *duration* of backwardation ([McMillan VX monitor](https://agent-jay-ser5.tail14ee01.ts.net/docs/vix-monitors/vx3-vx1-mcmillan.html); [thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/vix-term-structure)). Squeeze breakouts on stock universes show 52–55% win rates with payoff ratios of 1.06–1.14 ([QuantConnect](https://quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_defe22ea8f146d5740a1e68277c7affe.html)), and NR7 was negative in the in-house holdout.

### Regime, breadth and seasonal gates are cheap to test and mostly cut exposure

Arthur Hill and Cesar Alvarez's trend-filtered system trades SPY only when EMA(50) > EMA(200), buying when RSI(5) or RSI(10) drops below 30 and selling above 70. It reports **CAR over 7%, about 30% exposure and a win rate above 80%**, with trailing stops lowering returns ([StockCharts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-arthurhill-2016-10-using-breadth-for-trading-bias-and-overboughtoversold-setups---a-mean-reversion-system-for-spy-/)). The same article frames breadth as a bias filter: % of stocks above EMA(200) above 60% bullish, below 40% bearish, and % above EMA(20) below 30 as short-term oversold. No fetched source backtests breadth, advance-decline or new-high/new-low filters on SPY mean reversion with numbers. A free proxy is the share of the nine long-history sector SPDRs above their 200-day SMA, but with nine names it moves in 11% steps and is likely to duplicate SPY > SMA200. Its incremental value over that filter is the thing to measure.

Seasonal windows behave like gates too. Buying SPY at the close before a scheduled FOMC announcement and selling at the end of the meeting earns **CAGR about 4% and Sharpe about 0.5–0.6 while invested on about 5% of days** at 5 bp per side over 1993–2024. It was flat in 2016–2019 and looks stronger when VIX is high ([Quantseeker](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/trading-the-fed-the-pre-fomc-drift)). Kurov, Wolfe and Gilbert find the drift "essentially disappeared" after 2015 ([Finance Research Letters 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v40y2021ics1544612320315956.html)). The narrow turn-of-month window [0, +3] is no longer significant in US ETFs, while the wider [−3, +3] window still shows **about 5–12 bp a day of excess return but has "declined substantially"** over the past decade ([Quantseeker TOM](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/turn-of-the-month-strategies-do-they)). Halloween, by contrast, held out of sample in all 37 original countries through April 2011 ([Jacobsen & Zhang](https://www.bnains.org/backtest/periode/The_Halloween_Indicator_everywhere_and_all_the_time_-_Ben_Jacobsen_%26_Cherry_Y._Zhang.pdf)) and for about 14 more years in later work ([CFA Institute blog](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/?p=7086)). Standalone seasonal books cut drawdowns but trail buy-and-hold on CAGR, so their best use is as conditions on mean-reversion entries (trade, or double size, only in TOM or pre-FOMC windows) or as a small, near-uncorrelated book. No published numeric test of RSI(2)-inside-TOM exists; a TradingView "TOM on steroids" script is unvalidated ([TradingView](https://jp.tradingview.com/script/fknZA6qS-Turn-of-the-Month-Strategy-on-Steroids)).

## Positional overlays trade return for drawdown, and ensembles lift Sharpe

### The jump model is the best-documented regime filter

The statistical jump model of Shu, Yu and Mulvey is the most rigorous regime overlay found. Its features come only from the index's own returns: EWM downside deviation (halflife 10 days) and EWM Sortino ratios (halflives 20 and 60 days). A two-state model with jump penalty λ is trained on 3,000 days, refit every six months, and λ is chosen monthly by the best Sharpe over a trailing eight-year validation window. The signal at day t's close applies from day t+2, and costs are 10 bp one-way. Holding the S&P 500 in the bull state and cash otherwise gave, **out of sample over 1990–2023, CAGR 11.2% vs 10.2%, volatility 13.1% vs 18.2%, Sharpe 0.68 vs 0.48 and MaxDD −26.6% vs −55.2%**, beating a hidden Markov model filter at 0.54 ([arXiv 2402.05272](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05272v3)). The gain comes from sidestepping crash regimes, not from extra return, and the same qualitative result holds on the DAX and Nikkei ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05272v3)). The λ selection is itself tuning, so it must stay inside the walk-forward loop exactly as specified. Because it is computable from SPY data alone and already includes costs and delay, it is the natural gate for long-only mean reversion and the natural switch for a trend book.

### Momentum rotations have decayed, while canaries cut drawdowns at a cost

Time-series momentum was positive "for every asset contract" in Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen's 1985–2009 futures sample ([AQR](https://aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Time-Series-Momentum?aqrPDF=1)), but Huang, Li, Wang and Zhou find little asset-by-asset evidence and show it performs about the same as a historical-mean strategy that needs no predictability ([JFE 2020](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/time-series-momentum-is-it-there/)). Antonacci's dual momentum regressed to the median of its variants out of sample ([AlgoEdge/ReSolve](https://algoedgeinsights.beehiiv.com/p/is-gary-antonacci-s-global-equity-momentum-strategy-robust)) and a related portfolio has "continuously trailed its benchmark" since 2012 ([TuringTrader](https://turingtrader-com.web.app/portfolios/antonacci-dual-momentum)). Price Action Lab attributes the post-publication deterioration to selection bias and regime change ([Price Action Lab](https://priceactionlab.com/Blog/?p=68274)). Keller and Keuning's canary approach is the most-replicated cross-asset risk switch. In DAA, VWO and BND are canaries, and the cash fraction is b/B where b counts canaries with negative momentum and B = 2; the canary was chosen on 1926–1970 data with an out-of-sample period of 1994–2018 ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/strategic-allocation/multi-class-momentum-portfolio-with-canary-crash-protection/)). Replications of the later BAA variant come in below the paper, partly due to fill timing, total-return data and the choice of cash asset, and it has trailed its benchmark in most years ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/14190/bold-asset-allocation-baa-keller/); [TuringTrader](https://turingtrader-com.web.app/portfolios/keller-bold-asset-allocation)). Credit-spread, dollar, gold/copper and yield-curve filters had no quantitative evidence this round and remain open hypotheses.

The in-house positional results match this picture. A 5-ETF Faber 10-month SMA book earned a holdout Sharpe of 0.73 against 0.68 for buy-and-hold with a −16% vs −27% drawdown, but the Sharpe ranged from **0.50 to 0.92 across the 21 possible rebalance days**. The ATH-plus-10×ATR breakout on 42 ETFs reproduced the paper's trade shape (+0.47R expectancy) yet earned only 4–7% a year as a portfolio ([prior backtest results](../research_notes/Price%20action%20strategies%20by%20horizon/backtest_results_price_action.md)). Timing luck of that size means rebalance-day choice must be treated as a trial dimension, or averaged across all offsets.

### Combining books is where hybrids pay most

Low-correlation streams raise Sharpe mainly by cutting volatility. A 50/50 blend of a trend system and a long-only SPY mean-reversion system reached **Sharpe 0.91 against 0.65 for each alone**, with volatility falling to 12.4% ([Price Action Lab](https://priceactionlab.com/Blog/?p=67828)). Trend plus covered calls across asset classes gave 0.86 vs 0.66 ([CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/trend-following-and-covered-calls-in-combination)). Volatility targeting reduces the left tail and vol-of-vol in all asset classes ([Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)), but vol-managed portfolios do not systematically beat unmanaged ones once parameters are estimated in real time ([Cederburg et al., JFE 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:1:p:95-117)). The in-house combined swing book (three mean-reversion rules on seven ETFs) reached a **holdout Sharpe of 0.72 with a −8% drawdown**, against SPY's 0.87 and −34% ([prior backtest results](../research_notes/Price%20action%20strategies%20by%20horizon/backtest_results_price_action.md)). That book was all one style. The more promising ensemble stacks different mechanisms: SPY/QQQ mean reversion gated by the jump model, a trend book (jump model or SMA10), the QQQ Noise Area intraday book, and a small pre-FOMC/TOM seasonal book, each sized to equal ex-ante volatility and scaled together to a 10–12% volatility target with overlapping SPY positions netted. Low-exposure books must earn T-bill interest on idle cash in the backtest, or their Sharpe and CAGR are misstated.

## Costs and statistics have to be stricter than the published backtests

Quantpedia's review of 671 replicated strategies finds out-of-sample Sharpe **33% lower on average and 44% lower at the median**, and McLean and Pontiff's figures (as summarised there) show returns 58% lower in the five years after publication ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-analysis-of-trading-strategies/)). Falck, Rej and Thesmar find publication date is the main predictor of decay, driven by crowding and multiple testing ([arXiv 2105.01380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)). A pipeline that ranks 40–80 hybrid variants on the same few ETFs will reproduce that decay unless it penalises the search itself.

### Model every cost per side, then stress it

The cost model per side is commission plus exchange and regulatory fees plus half-spread plus slippage, plus borrow for shorts. IBKR Pro Tiered charges **$0.0005–0.0035 per share with a $0.35 minimum and pass-through fees**, and Fixed charges $0.005 per share with a $1 minimum ([IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.ca/en/pricing/commissions-home.php)). The only measured slippage anchor is Concretum's **$0.001 per share across more than 1,000 live SPY market orders** ([Beat the Market PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)). For SPY and QQQ the quoted spread is one cent, below what daily-bar estimators can resolve, so a half-spread of $0.005 plus 0.5–1 bp slippage is a reasonable default. For thinner sector, country and leveraged ETFs, estimate half-spreads from daily bars with Abdi-Ranaldo, which uses close, high and low prices ([RFS 2017, IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v30y2017i12p4437-4480..html); [bidask R package](https://rdrr.io/cran/bidask/man/spread.html)), with Corwin-Schultz or EDGE as a cross-check. Market-on-close and market-on-open orders avoid the spread but carry auction deviation of perhaps 0.5–2 bp, and they are only legitimate if the signal is computed before the auction cutoff; using the official close in a signal that then trades at that close is look-ahead. Shorts on liquid ETFs typically pay general-collateral borrow.

Report every strategy's net Sharpe at **0x, 1x, 2x and 3x the base cost** and the break-even multiple k* where net Sharpe hits zero. A reasonable gate, proposed here rather than taken from a source, is net Sharpe above zero at 2x cost, k* of at least 3 for daily and weekly rules and at least 2 for intraday rules. The slippage sweep has already separated survivors from failures once: the QQQ ORB broke even at 2.24¢ per share while the QQQ Noise Area stayed above Sharpe 0.6 at 3¢ ([prior backtest results](../research_notes/Price%20action%20strategies%20by%20horizon/backtest_results_price_action.md)). Fill sensitivity belongs in the same layer. Re-run each rule with close fills, next-open fills, adverse fills of one tick or half-spread, and a one-bar delay for intraday rules, and require the worst case to keep at least half the base Sharpe.

### Test the whole family, not the winner

Every configuration ever run, including discarded parameter sweeps and the earlier round's 992 swing and 18 intraday variants, goes into a trial ledger. Three families of tests then use it.

**Family-wise bootstrap tests.** The `arch` Python package implements Hansen's SPA, Romano-Wolf StepM and the Model Confidence Set on a T × k loss matrix (loss = −net excess return), with a stationary bootstrap ([arch SPA docs](https://arch.readthedocs.io/en/latest/multiple-comparison/generated/arch.bootstrap.SPA.html); [arch reference](https://arch.readthedocs.io/en/latest/multiple-comparison/multiple-comparison-reference.html)). SPA's "upper" p-value is White's Reality Check, which is conservative and sensitive to poor models. The "consistent" p-value, which recentres only near-benchmark models, is Hansen's recommended version ([arch SPA docs](https://arch.readthedocs.io/en/latest/multiple-comparison/generated/arch.bootstrap.SPA.html)). The practical recipe is SPA consistent p < 0.05 to establish that *anything* beats the benchmark, StepM at 5% to name *which* strategies do, and MCS at 10% for the shortlist that cannot be told apart from the best. Run it against zero excess return and against buy-and-hold of the same ETF, per horizon and pooled, and treat the pooled run as the honest one.

**Per-strategy deflation.** The Deflated Sharpe Ratio adjusts for the expected maximum Sharpe of N unskilled trials, using the cross-trial variance of Sharpe ratios, skew, kurtosis and track length ([Bailey & López de Prado, SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). N should be the *effective* number of independent trials, which ml4t-diagnostic estimates by effective rank of the return correlation matrix ([ml4t-diagnostic guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ml4t/diagnostic/main/docs/user-guide/statistical-tests.md)). Harvey and Liu's haircut Sharpe converts Sharpe to a t-statistic, adjusts its p-value for M tests with Bonferroni, Holm or BHY, and converts back. A t of 3.0 can fall to about 2.0 after adjustment, and the haircut is non-linear: weak strategies lose most of their Sharpe while strong ones are penalised moderately ([Harvey & Liu, Backtesting](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf)). The reference code is R ([quantstrat haircut.Sharpe.R](https://rdrr.io/github/braverock/quantstrat/src/R/haircut.Sharpe.R)), so it has to be ported. The earlier round already shows how binding this is: the best of 992 swing variants had a Deflated Sharpe probability of 0.897, and the best intraday variant 0.947 ([prior backtest results](../research_notes/Price%20action%20strategies%20by%20horizon/backtest_results_price_action.md)). Adding 40–80 hybrid variants will push both further down unless the new rules are genuinely stronger.

**Probability of backtest overfitting.** CSCV splits the T × N return matrix into S even blocks, evaluates every combination of S/2 training blocks against the complementary test blocks (S = 16 gives 12,870 splits), and records where the in-sample winner ranks out of sample. PBO is the share of splits where it lands below the median. The authors suggest rejecting at PBO > 0.05, and the same machinery gives a performance-degradation slope and a probability of out-of-sample loss ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)). The earlier round's evidence that RSI(2) is not overfit came from exactly this test, a PBO of 4% across 15 variants ([EmpiricaLab](https://empiricalab.substack.com/p/connors-rsi2-spy)). PBO should be run once per rule family across its parameter grid, and once across all rules, on pre-holdout data only. Blocks need enough data for a stable Sharpe, so S = 8–10 suits shorter daily samples and S = 8 suits weekly rules, while intraday rules are aggregated to daily P&L first. DSR answers "is this Sharpe significant given the search?", while PBO answers "does the selection process pick winners that persist?"; they are complements, not substitutes ([ml4t-diagnostic guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ml4t/diagnostic/main/docs/user-guide/statistical-tests.md)).

### Quantify uncertainty and robustness as single numbers

For each shortlisted rule, a stationary-bootstrap 95% confidence interval on annualised Sharpe should have a lower bound above zero. Lo showed serial correlation can overstate annualised Sharpe by as much as 65% ([Lo 2002, FAJ](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2002/the-statistics-of-sharpe-ratios)), so mean-reversion and trend series need autocorrelation-adjusted Sharpe and Newey-West t-statistics. The Minimum Track Record Length gives the number of observations needed to assert that true Sharpe exceeds a reference ([Bailey & López de Prado, Journal of Risk](https://www.risk.net/journal-risk/2223785/sharpe-ratio-efficient-frontier)). Applied to a two-to-three-year holdout, it will usually show that a Sharpe below about 1.5 cannot be confirmed, which should be stated rather than glossed over. Ledoit and Wolf's studentised bootstrap test of Sharpe differences replaces the Jobson-Korkie test, which is invalid under fat tails and serial dependence ([Ledoit & Wolf 2008](https://uzh.ch/cmsssl/econ/dam/jcr:ffffffff-935a-b0d6-0000-00007214c2bc/jef_2008pdf.pdf)), but O'Connor reports it can be unreliable and has low power ([Econ Journal Watch 2024](https://econjwatch.org/file_download/1300/OConnorMar2024.pdf)), so it is information rather than a gate. Walk-forward efficiency, parameter plateau ratio, block-bootstrap 95th-percentile drawdown, share of positive years, Sharpe by VIX tercile, Newey-West alpha versus SPY and capacity at a set impact cost each reduce to one scalar for the scorecard. The thresholds for these are practitioner conventions proposed here, not published standards.

### A gated scorecard picks the book

No published standard composite exists, so the scorecard below is this report's synthesis. It works in four stages. Stage 0 is hygiene: a complete trial ledger, a sealed holdout, base costs, and at least 100 trades for intraday and swing rules or 30 for weekly rules. Stage 1 applies hard gates on pre-holdout data. Stage 2 runs the sealed holdout once. Stage 3 ranks survivors on a composite dominated by out-of-sample, cost-stressed and deflated quantities. Stage 4 picks the final book.

| Gate | Test | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Net Sharpe at 1x cost and bootstrap 95% CI | Sharpe > 0.5, lower bound > 0 |
| G2 | Net Sharpe at 2x cost; break-even multiple k* | > 0; k* ≥ 3 daily/weekly, ≥ 2 intraday |
| G3 | Deflated Sharpe with N_eff from full ledger | ≥ 0.95 (0.90–0.95 watchlist) |
| G4 | Harvey-Liu haircut Sharpe (BHY, M = all trials) | > 0, haircut ≤ 50% |
| G5 | PBO of the family the rule came from | ≤ 0.30 (≤ 0.10 green) |
| G6 | Romano-Wolf StepM vs zero excess; family SPA | Included; SPA consistent p < 0.05 |
| G7 | Parameter plateau ratio | ≥ 0.7 |
| G8 | Walk-forward efficiency | ≥ 0.5, stitched OOS Sharpe > 0 |
| G9 | Worst fill or one-bar delay | ≥ 50% of base Sharpe and > 0 |
| G10 | Year and regime consistency | ≥ 60% positive years, no year > 40% of P&L, positive in 2 of 3 VIX terciles |
| H1 | Sealed holdout | Net Sharpe > 0 and ≥ 0.5 × in-sample Sharpe |
| H2 | Holdout drawdown | ≤ block-bootstrap 95th-percentile MaxDD |

Among survivors, convert each metric to a percentile rank and score them as 0.25 × holdout net Sharpe + 0.15 × DSR + 0.10 × (1 − PBO) + 0.10 × StepM/haircut significance + 0.10 × Sharpe at 2x cost + 0.10 × stability (plateau, walk-forward efficiency, positive years) + 0.10 × Newey-West alpha t + 0.05 × drawdown-to-CAGR + 0.05 × low correlation to already-selected rules. Raw in-sample Sharpe and CAGR get no weight because they are already inside the deflated figures. Re-rank under equal, holdout-only and deflation-heavy weights and keep only rules that stay in the top quintile under all of them. Then take survivors inside the Model Confidence Set and add them greedily by score with pairwise daily correlation below 0.5, optionally weighting by (1 − PBO) × inverse volatility, which follows Bailey et al.'s suggestion to use PBO as a weight ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)). For "discovery" claims, a Newey-West t of at least 3 is a sensible extra hurdle, in line with Harvey and Liu's multiple-testing adjustment ([Harvey & Liu](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The research changes what "a better price-action strategy" means. The search for a new pattern has been run many times and mostly failed; the candlestick, squeeze, NR7 and pure VWAP rules die on contact with costs or replication. What survives replication are rules where an outside variable decides *when* or *what* to trade: relative volume choosing the stock, VWAP deciding when a momentum day has failed, a regime model deciding when long-only exposure is dangerous. The extra ingredients rarely add return on their own. They add it by making a rule trade less often in bad conditions or by combining with a book whose losses fall on different days. The most valuable near-term experiments are therefore not new rules but **ablations and gates on the two rules that already work**: does the VWAP-stop momentum book generalise beyond QQQ, and does a jump-model or VIX gate lift daily mean reversion enough to justify its low exposure?

The second shift is in how strategies are judged. The earlier round's best rules already sit near the Deflated Sharpe line (0.897 swing, 0.947 intraday), and every hybrid variant added makes that line harder to cross. A gate or filter that looks good after twenty cuts of the same SPY history is worth little unless it survives family-wise tests, PBO and a holdout it never saw. The likely outcome of honest testing is a small book of three or four modest, uncorrelated streams, each with a Sharpe well under 1.5 that the holdout cannot fully confirm. That is a realistic target, and it is better than one spectacular backtest that the scorecard would reject.
