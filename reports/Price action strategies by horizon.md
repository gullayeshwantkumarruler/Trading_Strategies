# Start with daily ETF mean reversion

The price-action strategies with the most credible evidence for US stocks and ETFs are not the most exciting ones. The best-supported rules are **short-horizon mean reversion on index ETFs at the daily-bar swing horizon**: RSI(2) below 10 above the 200-day average, low Internal Bar Strength (IBS), and large-N-day-decline pullbacks. They survive robustness checks, they are cheap to trade, and they can be tested end to end today with free yfinance daily data. At the intraday horizon the evidence favours **market intraday momentum**: last-half-hour momentum, and Concretum's SPY "Noise Area" breakout with a VWAP trailing stop. The heavily marketed 5-minute opening-range breakout (ORB) on QQQ reproduces gross, but in two independent replications it is worth about zero after roughly 2 cents per share of slippage. At the positional horizon, all-time-high breakouts with a 10x ATR trailing stop are the only clearly return-adding rule with out-of-sample support. They need survivorship-free single-stock data and roughly $1M or more of capital to beat costs. Index trend filters (10-month SMA, 50/200 cross) mainly cut drawdowns rather than add return. Candlestick patterns, Donchian breakouts on stocks, NR7/inside-bar squeezes and CNN chart patterns at weekly horizons have thin, paywalled or negative evidence. None of the headline results has independent peer-reviewed confirmation after 2023, so every build has to include its own sealed holdout period. The recommended order is: swing mean reversion on SPY/QQQ first (free data, week one); intraday Noise Area and last-half-hour momentum second (needs a free Alpaca key for 2016 onward minute bars); positional ETF trend filters third, with the single-stock ATH breakout deferred until survivorship-free data is available.

## Evidence ranks mean reversion and intraday momentum above breakouts

The research covered roughly 30 named strategies across three horizons. Ranked by quality and recency of evidence rather than by headline Sharpe, the field narrows quickly. The table below gives the verdict for each strategy that matters. "Evidence" means peer-reviewed or methodologically disclosed work with costs. "Author-only" means the result comes only from the originating authors, with no independent out-of-sample test.

| Intraday | SPY Noise Area + VWAP stop (Zarattini, Aziz, Barbon 2024) | Sharpe 1.24 at 1x, net of costs, 2007–2024 | Author-only, in-sample, fully specified | Build, verify on holdout |
| Intraday | 5-min ORB on Stocks in Play | Sharpe 2.81, CAGR 41.6% | Author-only, simplified fills | Build later (needs all-stock minute data) |

| Intraday | Overnight drift | ≈ zero in 2021–2025 | NY Fed 2026 | Skip |

The pattern across horizons is consistent. Rules with a known economic mechanism and simple, few-parameter specifications (option-dealer hedging flows intraday, liquidity-provision reversal at the daily close, trend persistence in single stocks over months) hold up. Pattern-recognition rules (candlesticks, chart shapes, squeezes) do not, and the extreme headline numbers come mostly from leverage, cost assumptions and in-sample selection. The sections below give the exact rules and the evidence behind each verdict.

## Intraday edges live in the last half hour, not the first five minutes

### Market intraday momentum has the deepest record

The academic anchor is the finding that the first half-hour return of SPY (previous close to 10:00) predicts the last half-hour return (15:30–16:00). The trading rule is simple: at 15:30, take the sign of the first-half-hour return and hold to the close. It was documented on 1993–2013 data ([Gao, Han, Li & Zhou working paper](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)) with an **in-sample R² of 1.6%** ([Li, Sakkas & Urquhart](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/95566/1/Accepted-Version.pdf)). Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens generalised it to more than 60 futures from 1974 to 2020. They used the "rest of day" return (previous close to 15:30) as the predictor, found it positive everywhere, and found the **1974–1999 and 2000–2020 subsamples similar**. They tied the effect to short-gamma hedging by option dealers and leveraged ETFs. They report gross results only, but note that the S&P 500 futures version stays positive at a 1-tick cost ([Baltussen et al., JFE 2021](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). Internationally the effect appears in **12 of 16 markets** over 2005–2017 ([Li, Sakkas & Urquhart](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/95566/1/Accepted-Version.pdf)). Barbon et al. show that large negative aggregate gamma relative to dollar volume produces end-of-day momentum, while large positive gamma produces reversal, and that both revert at the next open ([Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi & Moerke](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/5a99db31-0d37-4f86-9502-8cb0f3bff4fe/content)). No study found re-tests the SPY rule specifically after 2013, so a 2016–2026 test is new information rather than a replication exercise.

### The Noise Area strategy is the best-specified ETF rule

Concretum's "Beat the Market" paper turns the same mechanism into a full-day system, and its rules were read directly from the PDF. For each time of day HH:MM, σ is the 14-day average of |Close(HH:MM)/Open(9:30) − 1|. The upper bound is max(Open_t, Close_{t−1}) × (1+σ) and the lower bound is min(Open_t, Close_{t−1}) × (1−σ). The max/min term handles gaps. The strategy checks **only at HH:00 and HH:30** (first decision 10:00). It goes long above the upper bound and short below the lower bound. The improved version trails a stop at max(UB, session VWAP) for longs and min(LB, VWAP) for shorts, checked on the half-hour, and is flat at 16:00. Sizing is shares = AUM × min(4, 2%/σ_SPY,14d) / Open. Costs were $0.0035/share commission plus **$0.001/share slippage, a figure the authors measured from more than 1,000 live $100k SPY market orders** in April 2024 ([Beat the Market PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Beat-the-Market.pdf)).

Over 2007 to early 2024 the base model returned 6.2% a year with Sharpe 0.61 and a 21% drawdown. The VWAP-stop version at 1x leverage delivered **9.7% a year, 7.7% vol, Sharpe 1.24, 12% MaxDD, hit rate 43%, skew +1.29**. The vol-targeted version reached 19.6% a year with Sharpe 1.33 and beta −0.07, against SPY buy-and-hold Sharpe 0.45 and MaxDD 56% ([PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Beat-the-Market.pdf)). Sharpe rose with VIX at the open, consistent with the gamma mechanism. Two weaknesses matter. The authors admit the VWAP stop was added after they saw the base model fail, and the sample ends in early 2024 with no holdout ([PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Beat-the-Market.pdf)). The March 2024 to October 2026 period is therefore a free out-of-sample test, and it is the single most useful thing an intraday build can produce.

### Opening-range breakouts depend on stock selection and slippage

The QQQ 5-minute ORB goes long at 9:35 if the first 5-minute candle is bullish and short if bearish, with no trade on a doji. The stop sits at the opposite extreme of that candle and the target is 10R, otherwise the position exits at the close. Risk is 1% per trade with a 4x leverage cap. The paper reports Sharpe 1.13 on QQQ for 2016–2023 but **models no slippage** ([danfin.net summary](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)). An independent GitHub replication reproduces the gross result (Sharpe 1.06). With $0.02/share entry slippage, Sharpe falls to **0.23 and CAGR from 30.4% to 2.7%, with break-even near 2.2¢/share**. The 10R target hits on only 2–3% of trades ([giovannibrusco replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). A second replication on index CFDs for 2015–2026 finds no market net-positive with statistical confidence, and at most +0.06R gross on NQ in 2021–2026. It does find that the first candle carries about 0.10–0.13R of direction information, which is roughly the cost of a trade ([MQL5 replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). The leverage cap also binds on about 60% of trades, so reported returns depend heavily on margin rules ([danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)).

The "Stocks in Play" version is different in kind because the edge comes from selection. Each day it screens all US stocks for open > $5, 14-day average volume ≥ 1M, 14-day ATR > $0.50 and opening relative volume ≥ 100%, then trades the top 20 by relative volume. A bullish first candle places a buy-stop at the opening-range high and a bearish one a sell-stop at the low. The stop is 10% of ATR, the position exits at the close, and costs are $0.0035/share ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/intraday-trading-of-overactive-stocks-via-opening-range-breakout/)). It reports **Sharpe 2.81 and CAGR 41.6%**. The same rule on the unfiltered universe gets only Sharpe 0.48, and per-trade edge rises from −0.02R below 100% relative volume to +0.38R above 3,000% ([danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)). Edge collapses as the opening range widens: Sharpe is 1.43 at 15 minutes and 0.21 at 30 minutes. The results are in-sample, with no out-of-sample period and simplified spread, borrow and stop-fill modelling ([danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)). Testing it properly needs survivorship-free minute bars for every US stock, which is outside a free setup.

### The overnight drift has gone

The E-mini's 2–3 a.m. drift once supplied more than 60% of close-to-close returns. It **averaged about zero over 2021–2025**, and the NightShares overnight ETFs closed after about 14 months ([NY Fed Liberty Street, July 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)). For SPY as an ETF, the overnight premium is unstable across subperiods ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/spy-by-day-of-week-and-overnight/)) and is wiped out by costs as a stand-alone trade ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/trading-costs-wipe-out-the-overnight-return-anomaly/)). Gap-fade, VWAP mean reversion and first-hour reversal have no rigorous US-equity study with costs. They remain hypotheses. The most promising of them is fading the open after a large overnight move, since one 2025/26 paper finds a high overnight return negatively predicts the first half-hour ([Iwanaga & Sakemoto, SSRN 5807282](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5807282.pdf?abstractid=5807282)).

## Daily oversold-in-uptrend rules carry the strongest swing evidence

### RSI(2) is the best-documented case, and it has decayed relative to buy-and-hold

The canonical Connors rule buys SPY at the close when **RSI(2) < 10 and close > SMA(200)**, and sells at the close when **close > SMA(5)**. There is no price stop. The most rigorous recent test covers dividend-adjusted SPY from January 1993 to September 2026 at 2 bp per side. It finds **265 trades, 77% winners, +0.63% average trade, about 3-day holds, Sharpe 0.81 versus 0.65 for buy-and-hold, and MaxDD 14.8% versus 55.2%**, but only 5.0% CAGR versus 10.8%, because the strategy is in the market on about 11% of days ([EmpiricaLab](https://empiricalab.substack.com/p/connors-rsi2-spy)). The same test is unusually thorough on robustness. All 15 parameter variants (RSI length 2–4, threshold 5–25) are positive, and no random-entry simulation matched the rule. The bootstrap 95% CI on Sharpe is 0.53–1.11, the deflated Sharpe is 99%, and the probability of backtest overfitting is 4%. CAGR falls from 5.3% to 3.7% as costs rise from 0 to 10 bp per side ([EmpiricaLab](https://empiricalab.substack.com/p/connors-rsi2-spy)).

Decay is real but mostly relative. Comparing before and after 2009, the average trade fell from 0.79% to 0.51% and Sharpe from 0.97 to 0.68, while buy-and-hold Sharpe rose from 0.42 to 0.87 ([EmpiricaLab](https://empiricalab.substack.com/p/connors-rsi2-spy)). One low-weight replication found edge per trade shrank in the 2010s and recovered in 2020–2024, with a 2020+ holdout of 58 trades averaging +0.50% net ([GitHub research card](https://github.com/jeisenback/golden_boy_peanuts/issues/262)). On five other equity ETFs, Sharpe was only 0.38–0.53 ([EmpiricaLab](https://empiricalab.substack.com/p/connors-rsi2-spy)). The rule is therefore best judged as a low-exposure overlay, with cash earning T-bills and other signals stacked alongside, not as a replacement for holding the index.

### IBS adds a close-location filter that is specific to the US session

IBS = (Close − Low)/(High − Low). On US equity ETFs up to May 2013, the next-day return averaged **+0.35% when IBS < 0.2 and −0.13% when IBS > 0.8**. The effect was stronger in high volatility and after wide-range days, strongest on Mondays, absent on low-volume days, and largely absent in non-US-session index ETFs ([Pagonidis 2013](https://www.naaim.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/00V_Alexander_Pagonidis_The-IBS-Effect-Mean-Reversion-in-Equity-ETFs-1.pdf)). Pagonidis's combined rule buys at the close when **RSI(3) < 10**, holds while **RSI(3) ≤ 40**, and only enters or holds when **IBS ≤ 0.5**. The IBS filter removed about 43% of market days and raised total returns by about 9.6 percentage points ([Pagonidis 2013](https://www.naaim.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/00V_Alexander_Pagonidis_The-IBS-Effect-Mean-Reversion-in-Equity-ETFs-1.pdf)). Used alone after 2009, IBS is weak on the S&P 500: a long/short IBS rule on IVV over 2009–2019 managed only Sharpe 0.20 before costs ([Pandey & Joshi 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12434)). A practitioner QQQ rule (buy at close when IBS < 0.1, sell when IBS > 0.9) claims a +0.9% average trade and 70% win rate over about 30 years, but its costs and drawdown are not disclosed ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/the-ibs-indicator-the-powerful-indicator)). IBS should therefore be coded as a filter on RSI rules first and as a stand-alone rule second.

### Pullback families favour tech-heavy ETFs and next-open entry still works

The broadest recent test covered 6,000 backtests across 75 ETFs and 20 entry families, with **next-open entry, 10 bp round-trip costs and a roughly 70/30 holdout**. The best entry was a "large 5-day decline" (median profit factor about 1.55) and the best exit was "**close above the prior day's high**". By ETF, median PF was **MTUM 2.68, SMH 2.36, QQQ 2.31 and SPY 1.83**, while commodity, volatility and bond ETFs fell below 1 ([QuantifiedStrategies 75-ETF study](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/best-etf-for-mean-reversion-strategies)). Rankings shifted in the holdout, and exact thresholds were not disclosed, so the in-house build has to choose its own thresholds and log the trial count. Classic Connors specifications worth testing are three or more lower closes, or a close at a 7-day low, above the 200-day SMA, with an exit on close > SMA(5) or a 7-day-high close. They are book specifications, not verified results.

A key implementation question is close versus next-open entry, and no source measures it head to head. EmpiricaLab fills at the signal close, which live requires estimating RSI just before the bell and sending a market-on-close order ([EmpiricaLab](https://empiricalab.substack.com/p/connors-rsi2-spy)). Pagonidis recommends the closing auction to avoid the spread ([Pagonidis 2013](https://www.naaim.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/00V_Alexander_Pagonidis_The-IBS-Effect-Mean-Reversion-in-Equity-ETFs-1.pdf)). Lou, Polk and Skouras show that short-term reversal profits accrue overnight ([JFE 2019](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf)), which implies a next-open entry gives up part of the edge. The backtest should report both conventions.

### Breakout and pattern rules lack swing-horizon support

Candlestick evidence on US stocks is old and mostly negative. Twenty-eight signals on DJIA stocks over 1992–2002 showed no predictive power under bootstrap tests ([CXO summary of Marshall, Young & Rose](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/candlesticks-fiddlesticks/)). Lu, Chen and Hsu found eight 3-day reversal patterns profitable at 0.5% cost after data-snooping correction, but **only under one holding rule and not the other** ([J. Banking & Finance 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426615002678)). In other words, the exit rule drives the result, not the pattern. Practitioners report that Donchian channels work for commodities and currencies "but not for stocks" ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/donchian-channels-trading-strategy)). NR7 results on SPY are paywalled ([QuantifiedStrategies NR7](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/nr7-trading-strategy-the-narrow-range-b35)). None of these deserves build time before the mean-reversion core is done.

## Positional returns come from rare single-stock outliers, while index filters mainly cut drawdowns

### All-time-high breakout with a 10x ATR stop is the one return-adding rule

The Wilcox-Crittenden rule, updated by Zarattini, Pagani and Wilcox (2025), is precise. A stock qualifies when its unadjusted close is above $10 and its 42-day average dollar volume is above a CPI-adjusted $1M. Entry is at the next open after a close at or above the highest adjusted close in the stock's history. The trailing stop is **ATH × (1 − 10 × ATR42/Close)**, ratcheted up and never lowered, and the position exits at the next open on a close below it ([Zarattini et al. 2025](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Does-Trend-Following-Still-Work-on-Stocks.pdf)). Across about 66,000 trades from 1950 to 2024, the **win rate is 43.9%, the average win +1.90R against an average loss of −0.70R**, and winners are held about 370 days. **Fewer than 7% of trades produce most of the profit**, and the trade statistics hold out of sample for 2005–2024 ([Zarattini et al. 2025](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Does-Trend-Following-Still-Work-on-Stocks.pdf); [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/does-trend-following-still-work-on-stocks/)).

The portfolio version buys every new ATH among Russell 3000 members. It weights each position at 30%/σ_i(42d) × 1/max(200, N), with gross leverage capped at 200%. Over 1991–2024 it earned **15.02% CAGR, Sharpe 0.85, MaxDD 31.75% and alpha 6.19%/yr gross**, against a market CAGR of 11.18% and MaxDD of 54.68% ([Zarattini et al. 2025](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Does-Trend-Following-Still-Work-on-Stocks.pdf)). Costs change the picture sharply with account size. **At $100k AUM, net CAGR is 2.45% with Sharpe 0.06, because commissions drag returns by more than 10%/yr. At $10M, net CAGR is 12.53% with Sharpe 0.70** ([Zarattini et al. 2025](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Does-Trend-Following-Still-Work-on-Stocks.pdf)). A small account holding few names can also miss the few outliers that drive returns ([Harbourfront Quant](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/does-trend-following-work-on-single)). Survivorship bias is acute for this strategy. The credible studies use Norgate's survivorship-free data with point-in-time membership. Tests on current index members both drop delisted losers and include future winners before they joined the index ([Enlightened Stock Trading](https://enlightenedstocktrading.com/stock-trading-systems-survivorship-bias-problems)). yfinance cannot supply delisted tickers, so any free single-stock test of this rule is biased upward by an amount no source has quantified.

### Trend filters are drawdown insurance, not alpha

Faber's rule holds the asset when the month-end close is above its 10-month SMA and T-bills otherwise. On the S&P 500 over 1901–2012 it improved return only modestly (**10.2% versus 9.3%**) but cut volatility from 17.9% to 12.0% and MaxDD from −83.5% to −50.3%. After publication (2006–2012) it beat buy-and-hold in only 3 of 7 years, mostly by avoiding 2008. CXO warns the 10-month choice carries data-snooping risk ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1115)), and results move with the day of the month chosen for rebalancing ([Newfound Research](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2013/08/the-luck-of-the-rebalance-timing/)). The 50/200-day golden cross shows the same profile. Since 1960 its CAGR is 6.8% versus 7.2% for buy-and-hold ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/golden-cross-trading-strategy-backtest)). On SPY over 2010–2024 it still suffered a −33.7% drawdown when the 2020 crash outran the 200-day lag ([vankyle00](https://vankyle00.github.io/trading-models/docs/models/01-sma-crossover-spy.html)). These filters should be judged by Sharpe, MAR and drawdown, not by CAGR.

The other positional candidates are weaker. George and Hwang's 52-week-high ranking (long the top 30% by P/52-week-high, hold 6 months) earned 0.45%/month over 1963–2001 ([George & Hwang 2004](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/tgeorge/papers/gh4-paper.pdf)), but turned **significantly negative in the US over 2001–2014** ([Lobão & Fernandes 2017](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cuf:journl:y:2017:v:18:i:2:lobao)). CNN image-pattern models show "no remarkable advantage" at one-week horizons, and transferring them to monthly holding raises Sharpe only modestly ([Zhu & Zhu 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08483)). Darvas boxes have no peer-reviewed US test, and the one published simulation used survivorship-biased Nasdaq-100 members and suffered a 66% drawdown ([Traders' Tips 2005](https://traders.com/documentation/feedbk_docs/2005/06/TradersTips/TradersTips.html)).

## Backtests fail on fills, data feeds and trial counts

### Data: yfinance is enough for swing, and Alpaca is required for intraday

yfinance daily bars cover SPY since 1993 and QQQ since 1999 at no cost, which is enough for every swing and ETF-level positional test. Intraday Yahoo data is limited. **2m–30m bars cover only the last 60 days** ([yfinance issue #2451](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/2451)), **1m bars cover about 30 days in roughly 7-day chunks** ([yfinance issue #2128](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/2128)), and the commonly cited 1h limit of about 730 days has no primary source. The best free multi-year source is **Alpaca's free Basic plan, which serves minute bars from 2016 onward**, at 200 historical calls per minute ([Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/about-market-data-api)). Alpaca staff say only the most recent 15 minutes of consolidated SIP data is excluded on the free plan, and that users must request **`feed=sip`** to avoid silently getting IEX-only bars ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/cant-read-spy-minutes/14293)). This matters because IEX-only bars distort highs, lows and volume, which are the inputs to every price-action signal. Massive (ex-Polygon) free covers only 2 years ([Massive pricing](https://massive.com/pricing)), and Alpha Vantage's long intraday history is now premium ([Alpha Vantage docs](https://www.alphavantage.co/documentation/)). Pre-2016 minute data, for example to include 2008 in the Noise Area test, requires a one-off purchase. FirstRate sells SPY minute bars from 2000 ([FirstRate SPY](https://firstratedata.com/i/etf/SPY)), and new Databento accounts get $125 in credits ([Databento pricing](https://databento.com/pricing)). Using Alpaca means the user has to create a free account and supply the API key ID and secret. It cannot be done without them.

### Execution: the conservative conventions

The pitfalls that inflate price-action backtests are well known, and the fixes are mechanical. A signal computed from bar t's close, high or low fills at bar t+1's open. This is the default in backtesting.py, which fills at the current close only when `trade_on_close=True` ([backtesting.py docs](https://kernc.github.io/backtesting.py/doc/backtesting/backtesting.html)). Fills at the signal close are legitimate only if they model a market-on-close order using a pre-close estimate. Breakout entries are stop orders at levels known before the bar and fill at max(open, level). When a stop and a target both fall inside one bar, assume the stop hit first, or resolve the order with 1-minute data. A gap through a stop fills at the open, not the stop price. Limit fills require price to trade through the limit, because touch-fills flatter mean-reversion rules.

For costs, the measured anchors are $0.001/share of SPY market-order slippage in Concretum's live test, $0.0035/share IBKR-tier commission, and the ORB break-even near 2¢/share ([Beat the Market PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Beat-the-Market.pdf); [ORB replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). For daily ETF rules, 2 bp per side is the published baseline, with 6 and 10 bp as stress cases ([EmpiricaLab](https://empiricalab.substack.com/p/connors-rsi2-spy)). Every intraday result should be reported with a **slippage sweep from 0 to 5¢/share and the break-even slippage**, because that one number separated the surviving strategies from the dead ones in this research.

### Metrics: three layers, plus trial-count deflation

Report results in three layers. The first is return and risk on the daily equity curve: CAGR, annualised vol, Sharpe (computed on daily returns even for intraday systems), Sortino, Calmar/MAR, MaxDD and its duration, and Ulcer Index. The second is trade-level statistics: number of trades, win rate, payoff ratio, profit factor, expectancy in R and %, SQN, average hold, exposure, turnover and cost drag. The third is robustness: skew, kurtosis, bootstrap percentiles of CAGR and MaxDD from 5,000–10,000 trade resamples, and the **Probabilistic and Deflated Sharpe Ratios** using the logged number of variants tried ([Bailey & López de Prado 2014](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551)). The probability of backtest overfitting via CSCV is a useful addition when parameters are swept ([Bailey et al. 2015](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2326253)). backtesting.py's `run()` already outputs most of the trade layer, including profit factor, expectancy, SQN, Kelly, exposure and durations ([backtesting.py docs](https://kernc.github.io/backtesting.py/doc/backtesting/backtesting.html)). Add quantstats on the daily equity curve and short custom functions for PSR/DSR and the bootstrap. Python 3.13 wheel availability for backtesting.py, vectorbt and quantstats was not verified, so check it at install time. A plain pandas/NumPy vectorised engine is a safe fallback for the simple daily rules.

## A prioritised build-and-backtest plan per horizon

Every strategy gets one fixed rule set from the literature, a pre-committed cost model, and a **sealed holdout that is run exactly once**. Every parameter variant is logged so the Deflated Sharpe can be computed. The success thresholds below are proposed by this report, not taken from the sources. They are set so that a pass means the edge plausibly survives live trading.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual retail ordering. The strategies marketed hardest (5-minute opening-range breakouts, candlestick and chart patterns, Darvas boxes) have the weakest net-of-cost evidence. The quiet ones (buy an oversold index ETF at the close in an uptrend, or follow the day's direction into the last half hour) rest on identifiable mechanisms: liquidity provision at the daily close, and dealer and leveraged-ETF hedging flows intraday. The second lesson is that cost and data assumptions decided almost every verdict. One or two cents per share separates a Sharpe-1 ORB from a dead one. Ten thousand dollars of commissions separates an alpha-positive ATH system from a losing one. IEX versus SIP bars change the highs and lows a breakout keys on. Break-even cost and data provenance belong in the headline of every result, not in a footnote.

The test window is also unusually valuable now. Both of Concretum's flagship intraday papers stop in early 2024, RSI(2)'s post-publication decay is documented through 2026, and the overnight drift has just been shown to have disappeared. A disciplined in-house run of the Phase 1 and Phase 2 rules over 2024–2026, with one fixed rule set, a logged trial count and a sealed holdout, would produce out-of-sample evidence that does not yet exist in the published record. That is a better use of the next month than searching for new patterns.
